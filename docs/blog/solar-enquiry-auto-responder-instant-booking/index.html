Solar Enquiry Auto-Responders and Instant Booking for Australian Installers

By Rodney Lunt · 11 August 2026 · 10 min read

Share:

Here is the lead you just lost. Someone sat on the lounge on a Tuesday night, filled in the enquiry form on your website, and filled in two others while they were at it. One of those businesses had an automated reply land in their inbox within seconds, with a link to book a site assessment. They booked it before bed. You saw the enquiry on Wednesday afternoon when you came down off a roof, rang back twice, got voicemail, and never heard from them again.

You did not lose that job on price. You never got the chance to quote. You lost it in the gap between the enquiry landing and anyone in your business doing something about it. That gap is the most expensive thing in a solar business, and almost nobody measures it.

I have watched this pattern for twenty years, from the supplier side, the sales side, and the back office. The trade has changed enormously. Panels went from 165W to over 600W, we shifted from RECs to STCs, and quoting tools got far better. The one thing that has not changed is how fast a hot solar lead goes cold, and how few installers have built anything to catch it.

Why speed matters more in solar than almost anywhere

By the time a homeowner is filling in enquiry forms, the hard decision is already made. They have decided to buy solar. What they are choosing now is who installs it, and that choice firms up fast.

Research into online lead response consistently points the same way: the odds of actually connecting with a prospect collapse within minutes of the enquiry, not hours, and businesses that respond first win a disproportionate share of the work (Oldroyd et al., 2011). You do not need the exact percentages to act on this. You already know it from your own quotes. The customer who rang back impressed says “you were the only one who got back to me”, and that is not a comment about your pricing.

Solar adds two pressures on top of the usual race. The first is that enquiry volume spikes hard around triggers: a state scheme announcement, a feed-in tariff change, a power bill landing after a hot summer. When everyone enquires at once, the business that can absorb that spike without dropping leads cleans up, and the business relying on manual callbacks drowns.

The second is the certificate clock. Small-scale Technology Certificates (STCs) are not a rebate. They are tradeable certificates, created from an eligible system’s deemed generation, that liable electricity retailers must buy and surrender to meet obligations under the Renewable Energy Target (Clean Energy Regulator, n.d.). The customer assigns their right to create those certificates to you in exchange for an up-front discount. Because the STC price floats with the market and the deeming period steps down over time, “sooner” is genuinely worth money to the customer. That is a legitimate reason to move quickly, and it is a reason you can build into your follow-up without inventing urgency.

What an enquiry auto-responder actually needs to do

Most installers who have anything at all have a single auto-reply that says “thanks, we have received your enquiry and will be in touch”. That is better than silence, barely. It acknowledges receipt and then asks the customer to wait, which is the one thing they will not do.

A response that earns the job does three things at once.

It acknowledges instantly, by both email and SMS where you have the mobile. Solar enquiries come in from phones, and an SMS that lands while they are still on your site does more work than an email they will read tomorrow.

It carries local context. A line that shows you understand the Australian scheme, the current STC mechanics, or a relevant state programme tells the customer they are dealing with someone who knows the territory. Keep it accurate and general. Reference that certificate timing affects the price rather than promising a specific dollar amount an automated message cannot stand behind.

It gives one clear next step. Not “we will call you”, which hands control back to your busiest, most distracted resource. The next step should be an action the customer takes themselves: book a site assessment, confirm a quote time, pick a slot. The whole job of the message is to move the lead one concrete step forward while they are still warm.

Instant booking is the piece almost everyone skips

This is where the real leverage sits, and where most setups stop short.

An instant booking link lets the prospect lock in a site assessment or a quote call without waiting for a callback. You have seen the consumer version of this everywhere: a calendar that shows your real availability, the customer picks a slot, it is confirmed, and it drops into your diary. Calendly-style tools do exactly this, and some job management platforms build it in directly.

The difference it makes is structural, not cosmetic. With a callback model, every lead waits on a human being free at the right moment, and that human is on a roof for most of the working day. With a booking link, the lead converts itself into a confirmed appointment at the moment of highest intent, which is the instant they finish enquiring. You have turned the slowest part of your funnel into the fastest.

The detail that matters: the booking has to feed your actual schedule, not a separate calendar nobody checks. A confirmed site visit sitting in a standalone booking tool, disconnected from the job pipeline, just moves the manual re-entry problem one step down the line. The appointment, the customer’s details, and the eventual quote should travel together as one thread, not get keyed in three times across three tools.

The tools, and where each one falls short

There is no shortage of software pointed at this problem, but most of it solves one slice and leaves the seams to you.

General-purpose autoresponders like AWeber (AWeber, n.d.) are genuinely good at what they do: sequences, triggers, list management. What they are not built for is the solar enquiry-to-install workflow. They will fire the email beautifully and then leave you to bridge the booking and the handoff into your job schedule by hand.

Solar-specific platforms like OpenSolar (OpenSolar, n.d.) bring proper solar context, design, and proposal tooling that a generic email tool never will. The gap to watch is the booking-and-intake layer at the very front of the funnel, and whether a confirmed appointment routes cleanly into the rest of your operation rather than stalling at the proposal stage.

The honest summary is that most installers end up stitching together a form tool, an email autoresponder, a booking calendar, and a job tracker, then spending their evenings copying information between them. Every handoff between those tools is a place a lead can quietly fall through, and busy weeks are exactly when it happens most.

Connecting capture to the job pipeline

The point of all of this is not a tidier inbox. It is that a booked lead lands in your scheduling and quoting workflow automatically, so nothing depends on someone remembering to move it across.

Map your own flow once, end to end: enquiry in, instant acknowledgement out, booking link offered, slot confirmed, appointment in the schedule, quote raised, follow-up sequence running. Then find every point in that chain where a human currently has to manually carry the lead from one tool to the next. Those carry points are your leak points. The goal is to remove them, not to add another app on top.

This is exactly the gap I am building CurrentFlow to close: lead intake, auto-response, and scheduling in one place, with instant booking flows designed to route a confirmed appointment straight into the job queue so no lead sits uncontacted while the crew is on the tools. It is the tool I wanted when I was watching good businesses lose work they had already half-won. It is pre-launch, so for now treat the principles above as the playbook regardless of what software you run.

The mistakes that cost the most

Three failures account for nearly every lead lost at this stage, and all three are fixable in an afternoon.

The first is the generic response with no local context. A bland “we will be in touch” tells the customer nothing and reassures them about nothing. A few accurate lines about the scheme and the timing show competence and give a reason not to stall.

The second is no booking option. If the next step in your reply is “we will call you”, you have handed control of the timeline to your busiest resource and bet the job on them being free at the right moment. They will not be.

The third, and the most expensive, is the multi-day delay before any human contact. The enquiry sits in an inbox while everyone is on a roof, and by the time someone replies, a faster competitor has booked the assessment. Automating the first response and the booking is the only reliable way to remove the dependency on someone being free at the exact moment the lead comes in. Under Australian Consumer Law you still owe customers clear, accurate dealing once they engage (ACCC, n.d.), so build speed without overpromising. Fast and honest beats slow every time.

You will not win every job. But you should never again lose one simply because nobody got back to the customer in time. That is the cheapest problem in your business to fix, and the most expensive one to ignore.

FAQ

How quickly should a solar enquiry get an auto-response?

Instantly. The whole point of an auto-responder is that the acknowledgement goes out the second the form is submitted or the missed call lands, while the prospect is still on your website or still holding the phone. A reply that arrives an hour later has lost most of its value, because by then they have usually moved on to ringing the next business on their list.

Is an auto-responder enough on its own?

No. An acknowledgement that says “we got your enquiry” with no next step is just a polite dead end. The response has to carry a clear action, ideally a booking link that lets the prospect lock in a site assessment themselves, so the conversation moves forward without waiting for someone in your team to be free to ring back.

Should I mention STCs in the auto-response?

A light touch helps, because it shows you know the local scheme and it gives the customer a reason not to stall. Just get the language right. Small-scale Technology Certificates are tradeable certificates, not a government rebate, and their value floats with the market, so timing genuinely matters. Do not overclaim a fixed dollar figure in an automated message.

Will a generic email autoresponder do the job?

It will handle the email, but it leaves a gap at the booking and the handoff. General-purpose tools are built to send sequences, not to drop a confirmed appointment straight into your job schedule. You end up bridging that gap by hand, which is exactly where leads slip when the team is flat out on the tools.

What is the single most common mistake?

A multi-day delay before any human contact, usually because the enquiry sat in an inbox while everyone was on a roof. By the time someone replies, a faster competitor has already booked the site visit. Automating the first response and the booking removes the dependency on someone being free at the exact moment the lead comes in.
